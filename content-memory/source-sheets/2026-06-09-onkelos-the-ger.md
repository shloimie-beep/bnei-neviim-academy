# Onkelos the Ger and Amitai's Conversion Question

## Related Student Question

- Student: Amitai Kosofsky
- Accountability event: `bna_accountability_events #8`
- Question: "If a non-Jew can become a Jew, why can't a Jew become a non-Jew?"
- Use: parent/student follow-up source sheet, not final psak.

## Short Answer For Class

A non-Jew can join the Jewish people through gerut because Torah recognizes a
path to enter the covenant. Once someone is Jewish, either by birth or by valid
conversion, the sources treat that Jewish identity as permanent. A Jew can sin,
drift, or even rebel, but the halachic category is still "Israel" or "a Jewish
apostate," not a non-Jew.

## Source Sheet Entries

### 1. Onkelos converted and used pesukim to draw others close

- Citation: Avodah Zarah 11a:2
- Hebrew excerpt: "אוּנְקְלוֹס בַּר קְלוֹנִימוּס אִיגַּיַּיר... מַשְׁכִינְהוּ בִּקְרָאֵי, אִיגַּיּוּר"
- Explanation: The Gemara introduces Onkelos as a famous Roman convert. His
  conversion was not just personal; he used Torah verses to bring Roman troops
  sent after him to convert as well.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Avodah_Zarah.11a.2
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "Why can a non-Jew become a Jew?"

### 2. Onkelos's first argument: Hashem leads Israel

- Citation: Avodah Zarah 11a:3, quoting Exodus 13:21
- Hebrew excerpt: "הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא נְקַט נוּרָא קַמֵּי יִשְׂרָאֵל"
- Pasuk excerpt: "וַה׳ הֹלֵךְ לִפְנֵיהֶם יוֹמָם"
- Explanation: Onkelos argues that the King of kings personally leads Israel.
  Joining Israel means joining a people led by Hashem, not just choosing a
  useful role or label.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Avodah_Zarah.11a.3
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.13.21
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "What changes when someone becomes Jewish?"

### 3. Onkelos's mezuzah argument: Hashem guards His servants

- Citation: Avodah Zarah 11a:5, quoting Psalms 121:8
- Hebrew excerpt: "עֲבָדָיו מִבִּפְנִים וְהוּא מְשַׁמְּרָן מִבַּחוּץ"
- Pasuk excerpt: "ה׳ יִשְׁמׇר צֵאתְךָ וּבוֹאֶךָ"
- Explanation: Onkelos points to the mezuzah and says that, unlike a human
  king guarded by servants, Hashem guards His servants. This frames Jewish
  identity as a protected covenant relationship.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Avodah_Zarah.11a.5
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.121.8
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "Why would someone want to become Jewish?"

### 4. After immersion, a convert is Jewish in every respect

- Citation: Yevamot 47b:15
- Hebrew excerpt: "טָבַל וְעָלָה הֲרֵי הוּא כְּיִשְׂרָאֵל לְכׇל דְּבָרָיו"
- Explanation: The Gemara says that after the conversion immersion, the convert
  is like an Israelite for all matters. This is the core source for why valid
  gerut creates real Jewish status.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Yevamot.47b.15
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "What makes conversion permanent?"

### 5. If the convert turns back, he is still a Jewish apostate

- Citation: Yevamot 47b:15
- Hebrew excerpt: "יִשְׂרָאֵל מְשׁוּמָּד קָרֵינָא בֵּיהּ, וְקִידּוּשָׁיו קִידּוּשִׁין"
- Explanation: Even if a convert later turns away, the Gemara does not call him
  a non-Jew. It calls him a Jewish apostate, and his kiddushin still counts.
  This directly addresses why there is no parallel "conversion out" of Jewish
  identity.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Yevamot.47b.15
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "Why can't a Jew become a non-Jew?"

### 6. A Jew who sins is still called Israel

- Citation: Sanhedrin 44a:2
- Hebrew excerpt: "אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁחָטָא, יִשְׂרָאֵל הוּא"
- Explanation: The Gemara derives from Achan that even when Israel sins, the
  person remains "Israel." This supports the same idea for born Jews: sin can
  damage behavior and standing, but it does not erase Jewish identity.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Sanhedrin.44a.2
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "Why does Jewish identity stay?"

### 7. Rambam: conversion means entering the covenant

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Forbidden Intercourse 13:4
- Hebrew excerpt: "לְהִכָּנֵס לִבְרִית... וִיקַבֵּל עָלָיו עֹל תּוֹרָה"
- Explanation: Rambam describes gerut as entering the covenant and accepting
  the yoke of Torah. This helps explain the asymmetry: there is a Torah process
  to enter the covenant, but no Torah process to erase it afterward.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Forbidden_Intercourse.13.4
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "What is a convert entering?"

### 8. Rambam and Shulchan Aruch: even later idolatry does not undo gerut

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Forbidden Intercourse 13:17
- Hebrew excerpt: "יָצָא מִכְּלַל הָעַכּוּ״ם... נַעֲשָׂה כְּיִשְׂרָאֵל"
- Citation: Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah 268:12
- Hebrew excerpt: "חזר ועבד אלילים הרי הוא כישראל מומר"
- Explanation: Rambam and Shulchan Aruch codify the Gemara's rule: after a
  valid conversion, the convert has left the category of non-Jews. Even severe
  later sin makes him a Jewish apostate, not a gentile.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Forbidden_Intercourse.13.17
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Yoreh_De'ah.268.12
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "Why is there no conversion back?"

### 9. Ruth: conversion joins people and God

- Citation: Ruth 1:16
- Hebrew excerpt: "עַמֵּךְ עַמִּי וֵאלֹהַיִךְ אֱלֹהָי"
- Explanation: Ruth's words capture the heart of gerut: joining the Jewish
  people and joining their God. It is a whole-identity commitment, not just a
  private belief change.
- Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Ruth.1.16
- Linked question: Amitai #8, "What does a convert choose?"
