## Sheet 1 - What Is A Good Torah Question?

Class issue: The recording argues that many students learn many texts without
learning what a good question is, what a good answer is, and how answers answer
questions.

### Source 1.1 - A Shy Person Will Not Learn

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 2:5.
- Hebrew excerpt: `וְלֹא הַבַּיְשָׁן לָמֵד`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.2.5>
- Who discusses it: Hillel in Pirkei Avot.
- Explanation: Torah learning requires enough courage to ask when something
  does not make sense. A good question is not chutzpah; it is part of learning.
- Links to the recording: If students are not trained to ask, they may know the
  words but not own the sugya.

### Source 1.2 - Make A Rav And Acquire A Friend

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 1:6.
- Hebrew excerpt: `עֲשֵׂה לְךָ רַב, וּקְנֵה לְךָ חָבֵר`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.6>
- Who discusses it: Yehoshua ben Perachyah.
- Explanation: A question needs a rav and a chavrusa. The rav helps guide the
  answer; the friend helps test whether the answer really fits.
- Student use: After asking, "What is bothering me?", ask, "Who can help me
  check whether the answer solves the problem?"

### Source 1.3 - Torah Learning Sharpens Through Another Person

- Citation: Taanit 7a:8.
- Hebrew excerpt: `בַּרְזֶל בְּבַרְזֶל יָחַד`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Taanit.7a.8>
- Who discusses it: R. Chama bar R. Chanina.
- Explanation: Torah becomes sharper when learners challenge and clarify each
  other. A good question creates precision.
- Links to the recording: Students need guided back-and-forth, not only coverage.

### Source 1.4 - A Teacher Must Show The Reasoning

- Citation: Eruvin 54b:14-15.
- Hebrew excerpt: `חַיָּיב... לְהַרְאוֹת לוֹ פָּנִים`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Eruvin.54b.14-15>
- Who discusses it: R. Akiva.
- Explanation: The Gemara says a teacher must repeat until the student learns
  and must show the `panim`, the reasoning or face of the matter.
- Student use: A good answer does more than give a conclusion. It shows why
  that conclusion answers the question.

### Source 1.5 - Rashi: Not Just Words In The Mouth

- Citation: Rashi on Exodus 21:1:2.
- Hebrew excerpt: `לַהֲבִינָם טַעֲמֵי הַדָּבָר`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Exodus.21.1.2>
- Who discusses it: Rashi, based on Mekhilta.
- Explanation: Rashi says Moshe was not only to repeat the halacha until it was
  fluent, but to explain its reasons and meaning like a set table.
- Links to the recording: This directly supports the critique that fluency and
  coverage are not enough if students do not understand how the answer works.

### Source 1.6 - Midrash: Set It Before Them Like A Prepared Table

- Citation: Mekhilta DeRabbi Yishmael, Tractate Nezikin 1:1-2.
- Hebrew excerpt: `עָרְכֵם לִפְנֵיהֶם כְּשֻׁלְחָן עָרוּךְ`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Mekhilta_DeRabbi_Yishmael%2C_Tractate_Nezikin.1.1-2>
- Who discusses it: R. Akiva in the Mekhilta.
- Explanation: The Midrash is the root source behind Rashi's language. It says
  repetition alone is not enough; the material has to be placed before the
  students in an understandable, orderly way.
- Student use: A good answer should be "set out" so the learner can see the
  path from question to answer.

## Sheet 2 - Mishnah Coverage: Quantity And Depth

Class issue: The recording criticizes a system that covers many Mishnayos while
students may not learn how to understand, ask, and apply.

### Source 2.1 - Mishnah Has Its Proper Stage

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 5:21.
- Hebrew excerpt: `בֶּן עֶשֶׂר לַמִּשְׁנָה`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.5.21>
- Who discusses it: Pirkei Avot's age-stage teaching.
- Explanation: Mishnah has a real place in a Torah curriculum. The critique is
  not that Mishnah coverage is bad; it is that Mishnah should develop into
  deeper learning at the right stage.
- Links to the recording: Quantity can be a foundation, but not the whole goal.

### Source 2.2 - There Is A Place For Initial Girsa

- Citation: Avodah Zarah 19a:10.
- Hebrew excerpt: `לִיגְרֵיס אִינִישׁ... דְלָא יָדַע`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Avodah_Zarah.19a.10>
- Who discusses it: Rava.
- Explanation: The Gemara recognizes value in initial recitation/coverage even
  before full understanding. This protects the value of broad Mishnah exposure.
- Balance: Girsa is a beginning layer, not proof that the student can reason.

### Source 2.3 - Torah Must Include Deriving One Thing From Another

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Torah Study 1:11.
- Hebrew excerpt: `יוֹצִיא דָּבָר מִדָּבָר`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Torah_Study.1.11>
- Who discusses it: Rambam.
- Explanation: Rambam divides learning time among Written Torah, Oral Torah,
  and Gemara-level understanding: comparing, deriving, and knowing how law is
  built from what was learned.
- Links to the recording: A curriculum that stops at coverage may miss the
  thinking skill students need for future learning.

### Source 2.4 - Mishnah Without Reasons Is Dangerous

- Citation: Sotah 22a:4 with Rashi on Sotah 22a:1:3.
- Hebrew excerpt from Gemara: `קוֹרֵא וְשׁוֹנֶה וְלֹא שִׁימֵּשׁ`.
- Hebrew excerpt from Rashi: `טַעֲמֵי הַמִּשְׁנָה`.
- Sefaria URLs:
  - <https://www.sefaria.org/Sotah.22a.4>
  - <https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Sotah.22a.1.3>
- Who discusses it: Chazal and Rashi.
- Explanation: The Gemara says even one who reads and learns Mishnah can still
  be missing serious Torah depth if he has not served talmidei chachamim. Rashi
  explains that this means he lacks the reasoning of the Mishnah.
- Links to the recording: This is one of the strongest classic sources for the
  warning that text coverage can masquerade as understanding.

### Source 2.5 - Review Beyond Habit Builds Avodah

- Citation: Chagigah 9b:10-11.
- Hebrew excerpt: `מֵאָה פְּעָמִים... מֵאָה וְאֶחָד`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Chagigah.9b.10-11>
- Who discusses it: Hillel and Bar Hei Hei.
- Explanation: The Gemara distinguishes between normal review and review that
  pushes beyond habit. Quantity matters when it deepens mastery and avodah.
- Links to the recording: The issue is not number of Mishnayos by itself; the
  issue is whether the numbers build deeper ownership or just school compliance.

### Source 2.6 - Amount Is Not The Only Measure

- Citation: Berakhot 5b:14.
- Hebrew excerpt: `אֶחָד הַמַּרְבֶּה וְאֶחָד הַמַּמְעִיט`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.5b.14>
- Who discusses it: R. Yochanan comforting R. Elazar.
- Explanation: Chazal can value both the one who does much and the one who does
  less, if the heart is directed to Heaven.
- Student use: A student should be pushed to grow, but not reduced to a single
  quantity score.

### Source 3.3 - Learn From The Place The Heart Desires

### Source 3.4 - Tanya: Natural Diligence And `Marah Shechorah`

- Citation: Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 15:6-10.

- Links to the recording: The transcript's garbled `Mar or black b` appears to

### Source 3.5 - Going Beyond Nature Is The Work

- Explanation: Even a tzaddik who learns a lot has another level when he goes

  may be to grow one real step beyond your current habit.

## Sheet 4 - Motivation, Bribes, And Long-Term Desire

Class issue: The recording warns that relying on candy/bribes/grades can make a
student lose his desire to learn Torah.

### Source 4.1 - Do Not Serve Only For Reward

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 1:3.
- Hebrew excerpt: `שֶׁלֹּא עַל מְנָת לְקַבֵּל פְּרָס`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.3>
- Who discusses it: Antigonos of Socho.
- Explanation: Torah and mitzvos should not become a transaction where reward
  is the whole point.
- Links to the recording: Bribes are dangerous when they train a student that
  Torah is worth doing only because of the prize.

### Source 4.2 - Desire The Mitzvos, Not Only Their Reward

- Citation: Avodah Zarah 19a:4.
- Hebrew excerpt: `בְּמִצְוֹתָיו וְלֹא בִּשְׂכַר מִצְוֹתָיו`.

- Explanation: Chazal read the pasuk as delighting in the mitzvos themselves,
  not only in what one receives afterward.
- Links to the recording: The goal is that the student eventually wants Torah,
  not only the candy, grade, prize, or fear-based outcome.

### Source 4.3 - `Lo Lishmah` Can Be A Bridge

- Citation: Pesachim 50b:5.
- Hebrew excerpt: `מִתּוֹךְ שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמָהּ בָּא לִשְׁמָהּ`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pesachim.50b.5>
- Who discusses it: Rav Yehudah in the name of Rav.
- Explanation: Chazal recognize that a person may begin with imperfect motives.
  But the source says `from lo lishmah comes lishmah`; it is a bridge, not the
  intended final home.
- Review note: Which classroom incentives are healthy bridges, and which trap
  the child in external motivation, needs Shloimie/rav review.

### Source 4.4 - Rambam: Children Are Trained Gradually Toward Love

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Repentance 10:5.
- Hebrew excerpt: `מַרְגִּילִין אוֹתָן... בְּנַחַת`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Repentance.10.5>
- Who discusses it: Rambam.
- Explanation: Rambam says beginners and children may be taught through fear or
  reward at first, but as their knowledge grows they are gently trained toward
  serving Hashem from love.
- Links to the recording: The problem is not every reward; the problem is a
  system that never moves the student toward inner desire and love.

### Source 4.5 - Torah Delight Is The Long-Term Goal

- Citation: Psalms 1:2.
- Hebrew excerpt: `בְּתוֹרַת יְהֹוָה חֶפְצוֹ`.

- Hebrew excerpt: `וּבִלְבַד שֶׁיְּכַוֵּין לִבּוֹ`.
- Sefaria URL: <https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.5b.14>
- Who discusses it: R. Yochanan.
- Explanation: Direction of heart matters. This does not excuse laziness, but

## Student Worksheet Prompts

1. Pick one Mishnah. Write the strongest question you can find. A strong
   question should name the exact word, phrase, assumption, or contradiction
   that is bothering you.
2. Write one possible answer. Then write why the answer answers the question.
   If it only restates the Mishnah, it is not finished.
3. Mark the type of learning you did:
   - `Girsa`: I covered/recited/reviewed the text.
   - `Havanah`: I understood the meaning.
   - `Svara`: I can explain why the answer works.
   - `Maaseh`: I can name one real-life decision this Torah shapes.
4. Ask: "Where is my heart's doorway into this Torah?" Use Avodah Zarah 19a as
   permission to find a real entry point, not as permission to avoid discipline.
5. Ask: "What is one small step beyond my habit?" Use Chagigah 9b and Tanya 15
   to focus on your avodah, not someone else's natural gift.

## BNA Teaching Context

For BNA use, the transcript can be framed positively:

- Torah coverage is valuable when it becomes understanding, questions, review,
  action, and self-governance.
- Mishnayos are not the problem. A flat, prize-driven, test-only experience is
  the risk.
- A child who needs movement, examples, projects, discussion, or practical
  problem-solving is not outside Torah. He may need a different doorway into
  Torah.
- Rewards can help at the beginning, but they need a path toward love,
  curiosity, responsibility, and inner desire.
- A student's source sheet should train him to ask, answer, explain, and apply.
  Parent follow-up should focus on growth signals and questions, not dump the
  whole research sheet.

## Open Points For Shloimie / Rav Review

1. The transcript's claim that "40 to 50%" of children are in trouble should
   not be presented as a researched statistic unless Shloimie supplies evidence
   or chooses it as rhetorical framing.
2. The recording's phrase about natural inclination is likely Tanya ch. 15's
   `מרה שחורה` discussion, but the exact wording and classroom use should be
   reviewed before teaching it to students.
3. Curriculum decisions about how many Mishnayos to cover, when to slow down,
   and which boys need alternate modalities are practical chinuch decisions, not
   automated psak.
4. Reward systems need a clear policy: when are prizes useful `lo lishmah`
   bridges, and when do they damage intrinsic Torah desire?
5. Any parent-facing version should avoid labeling a child as "not a gadol" or
   "not a sefer learner." The usable parent language should be about interests,
   struggle signals, questions, and next growth experiments.
