# Student Visa Bank Account Options

## Bottom Line

Working assumption: this is about opening an Israeli bank account while holding
an Israeli A/2 student visa, because BNA is operating in Beit Shemesh.

An A/2 student visa does not appear to prevent opening a personal Israeli bank
account. The practical path is usually an in-person branch appointment with a
passport, valid visa, second photo ID, student card/enrollment letter, home
country taxpayer ID, local Israeli phone number, Israeli address/proof of stay,
and a clear explanation of source of funds.

For BNA financial infrastructure, the risk is not the existence of the bank
account. The risk is using a student-visa holder's personal account for school,
business, payroll, tuition, donations, or project revenue. Official visa sources
say A/2 holders are generally not permitted to work in Israel, and some bank
non-resident account conditions exclude Israel-linked business. That should be
reviewed with an Israeli accountant/lawyer and, where halachic framing matters,
with Shloimie/rav before using a personal student account for BNA operations.

This is source research and practical risk mapping, not final psak or legal/tax
advice.

## Practical Source Map

### Israeli Student Visa Rules

1. Source: Israeli Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Consulate General of Israel
   Southeast USA, "Entry visas for university or yeshiva students."
   - Found: The A/2 student visa is for study in Israeli schools, academic
     institutions, yeshivas, and Jewish Agency youth institutions. It is valid
     up to one year with multiple entries, and recipients are not permitted to
     work in Israel.
   - Requirements listed there include a valid passport, acceptance letter,
     proof of financial capability, health insurance, proof of residence in
     Israel, and other consular documents.
   - URL:
     <https://embassies.gov.il/atlanta/en/services/foreign-citizens/entry-visas-university-or-yeshiva-students>

2. Source: Tel Aviv University International, "Student Visa (A/2)."
   - Found: International students in programs of one semester or longer need
     an A/2 visa. TAU says A/2 recipients are generally not permitted to work in
     Israel. TAU notes a narrow work-permission path for qualifying TAU
     MA/MSc/PhD/postdoc teaching or teaching-assistant roles, and says an
     existing A/2 holder would need a new visa to add work permission.
   - URL: <https://international.tau.ac.il/student-visa>

### Opening A Personal Student Account

1. Source: Hebrew University International, "Local Bank Account."
   - Found: Scholarship students are required to open a local Israeli account;
     other international students may do so for convenience. HUJI instructs
     students to go to a branch with passport, second photo ID, student card,
     certificate of enrollment, valid visa, home-country taxpayer ID, and local
     cell phone number.
   - HUJI also warns that some Israeli banks prefer not to open accounts for
     visiting students because their stay is temporary and they may lack regular
     income deposits.
   - HUJI reports successful student account openings at branches of Bank
     Hapoalim, Post Office, Discount Bank, and Bank Leumi.
   - URL: <https://international.huji.ac.il/local-bank-account>

### Israeli Banking Regulatory Frame

1. Source: Bank of Israel, Proper Conduct of Banking Business Directive 422
   circular, March 26, 2023.
   - Found: Israeli banking supervision emphasizes that basic current-account
     service should not be refused unreasonably and that banks should not use a
     blanket refusal only because a client belongs to a category of customers.
     Each request must be examined on its facts, with risk controls where
     appropriate.
   - Important limit: this circular's amended definition of "client" explicitly
     names Israeli residents and foreign workers lawfully residing in Israel.
     It does not explicitly name A/2 foreign students, so do not overclaim a
     guaranteed student entitlement from this source alone.
   - URL: <https://www.boi.org.il/media/5npbfoje/h2745.pdf>

2. Source: Bank of Israel press release on current accounts in credit balance.
   - Found: Banks must provide written refusal reasons within five business
     days after a rejected request to open an account. Basic services listed
     include standing/direct-debit payments, debit card use, ATM/cash card use,
     branch information services, and online bank-account access, subject to
     applicable rules and reasonable risk controls.
   - URL:
     <https://www.boi.org.il/publications/pressreleases/%D7%94%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%94-%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9%D7%94-%D7%91%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%90-%D7%A4%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%97%D7%AA-%D7%97%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%A8-%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%91-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%AA-%D7%96%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%94%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%95/>

### Foreign-Resident Account Option

1. Source: Bank of Jerusalem, "Foreign resident account / חשבון תושב חוץ."
   - Found: Bank Jerusalem offers a foreign-resident account for people living
     overseas who maintain accounts, savings, or deposits in Israel. The Hebrew
     page says the applicant must satisfy conditions including: Israel is not
     the permanent home of the applicant or family; the applicant's business is
     not in Israel and not connected to Israel; and the applicant does not own
     or partner in a business operating from Israel.
   - Process/restrictions: applicant must come personally to one of the listed
     branches; a proxy cannot open it; a non-resident declaration is required
     and renewed every three years; residents of some countries may have
     activity restrictions depending on regulation and bank procedures.
   - URL: <https://www.bankjerusalem.co.il/current-account/abroad>
   - English URL:
     <https://www.bankjerusalem.co.il/en/forex/non-resident-account>

## Recommended Practical Path

1. If the need is a personal living-expense or scholarship account:
   - Book in-person appointments with branches that regularly handle foreign
     students.
   - Bring passport, valid A/2 visa, second photo ID, student card or enrollment
     certificate, home-country TIN/SSN, Israeli address/proof of residence,
     local Israeli phone, proof/source of funds, and any scholarship/support
     letter.
   - Ask for a basic shekel current account with debit/ATM card and online
     access, not credit/overdraft.
   - If refused, ask for the refusal in writing and the exact missing document
     or compliance concern.

2. If the need is BNA project infrastructure:
   - Do not make a student-visa personal account the default operating account.
   - Safer options to review: open the account under the actual BNA entity or
     licensed business structure; use an Israeli resident/authorized signatory;
     use an approved fiscal/admin arrangement; or get written bank/accountant
     approval before routing project revenue through a personal account.
   - Confirm whether incoming funds are tuition, donations, service revenue,
     reimbursements, salary, or family support. The banking and tax treatment
     changes materially.

3. If the applicant is truly a non-resident with permanent life abroad:
   - Ask banks about a foreign-resident account.
   - This may not fit BNA operating money because Bank Jerusalem's listed
     foreign-resident conditions exclude an Israel-linked business or ownership
     in a business operating from Israel.

## Open Questions For Shloimie / Rav / Professional Review

1. Who is the proposed account holder: adult, minor, Israeli citizen, foreign
   citizen, U.S. person, or other nationality?
2. Is the visa exactly A/2, or an A/2 with explicit work permission?
3. Is the account for personal expenses/scholarship support, or for BNA school
   operations?
4. Will funds include tuition, donations, parent payments, payroll, provider
   payments, refunds, or only family support?
5. Is there already an Israeli entity, amuta, osek, company, or fiscal sponsor
   that should hold the account instead?
6. Halachic review: if a workaround routes institutional funds through a
   personal account, ask a rav about honesty, tax/legal compliance, marit ayin,
   and dina d'malchuta concerns. The Sefaria sources above do not by themselves
   authorize a workaround.

## Working Conclusion

Yes, a personal Israeli bank account can often be opened by a student-visa
holder, but expect branch discretion and KYC friction. For project financial
infrastructure, use a properly authorized personal or entity account only after
bank/accountant/legal review; an ordinary A/2 student visa is not a clean basis
for running BNA business funds through the visa holder's personal account.
