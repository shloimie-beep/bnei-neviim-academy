## Question #42 - Amitai Kosofsky

- Question: "Were they able to choose something that wasn't kosher for the
  taste of the manna?"
- Topic: manna taste.
- Transcript source: content job #25, "Joshua's Conquest and Moses and Aaron's
  Death."
- Short answer: The verified sources clearly say the manna could carry many
  tastes and even that most foods' taste or texture could be experienced in it.
  I did not find a verified Sefaria source that directly says whether a
  non-kosher taste was included or excluded.

### Sources

1. Citation: Exodus 16:31
   - Hebrew excerpt: "וְהוּא כְּזֶרַע גַּד לָבָן וְטַעְמוֹ כְּצַפִּיחִת בִּדְבָשׁ"
   - Explanation: The Torah describes the basic taste of the manna as honey-like.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.16.31
   - Linked question: Amitai #42, manna taste.

2. Citation: Numbers 11:8
   - Hebrew excerpt: "וְהָיָה טַעְמוֹ כְּטַעַם לְשַׁד הַשָּׁמֶן"
   - Explanation: A second Torah description gives the manna an oily-rich taste,
     which becomes the basis for the Gemara's broader taste discussion.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Numbers.11.8
   - Linked question: Amitai #42, manna taste.

3. Citation: Yoma 75a:10
   - Hebrew excerpt: "טַעַם כׇּל הַמִּינִין טָעֲמוּ בַּמָּן... טַעַם חֲמֵשֶׁת הַמִּינִין הַלָּלוּ לֹא טָעֲמוּ בּוֹ"
   - Explanation: One view says all tastes were in the manna except the five
     foods named in the complaint; another view says those five had taste but no
     texture. This gives Amitai a real source for asking about limits.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Yoma.75a.10
   - Linked question: Amitai #42, whether manna taste had limits.

4. Citation: Yoma 75a:20
   - Hebrew excerpt: "אַף הַמָּן... מוֹצְאִין בּוֹ כַּמָּה טְעָמִים"
   - Explanation: Rabbi Abbahu says the manna produced many tastes, and another
     version says it changed into many tastes. This supports the class premise,
     while leaving the non-kosher-taste question open.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Yoma.75a.20
   - Linked question: Amitai #42, many tastes in the manna.

### Optional Follow-Up

1. Read Yoma 75a:10 and list the two opinions about the five foods.
2. Ask: If the manna tasted like something, was that halachically the same as
   eating that thing?
3. Bring one follow-up about whether "taste" and "food identity" are the same.

## Question #43 - Amitai Kosofsky

- Question: "If the most righteous person has pride, is he still considered the
  biggest tzaddik?"
- Topic: pride, humility, and righteousness.
- Transcript source: content job #25.
- Short answer: The sources treat gaavah as a severe spiritual flaw. A tzaddik
  can still have something to fix, but pride is not a small detail; the Torah's
  model of greatness is Moshe Rabbeinu's deep humility.

### Sources

1. Citation: Proverbs 16:5
   - Hebrew excerpt: "תּוֹעֲבַת יְהֹוָה כׇּל־גְּבַהּ־לֵב"
   - Explanation: The pasuk calls haughtiness an abomination to Hashem, matching
     the class language about gaavah being "toevah."
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.16.5
   - Linked question: Amitai #43, severity of pride.

2. Citation: Sotah 5a:10
   - Hebrew excerpt: "כׇּל אָדָם שֶׁיֵּשׁ בּוֹ גַּסּוּת הָרוּחַ רָאוּי לְגַדְּעוֹ כַּאֲשֵׁירָה"
   - Explanation: Chazal compare arrogance to an idolatrous tree being cut down.
     This supports the class point that gaavah can resemble serving oneself.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Sotah.5a.10
   - Linked question: Amitai #43, gaavah and avodah zarah imagery.

3. Citation: Numbers 12:3
   - Hebrew excerpt: "וְהָאִישׁ מֹשֶׁה עָנָו מְאֹד"
   - Explanation: Moshe, the greatest teacher, is described by the Torah through
     humility. This gives a positive model of greatness without self-inflation.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Numbers.12.3
   - Linked question: Amitai #43, tzaddik and humility.

4. Citation: Mishneh Torah, Human Dispositions 2:3
   - Hebrew excerpt: "וְיֵשׁ דֵּעוֹת שֶׁאָסוּר לוֹ לָאָדָם לִנְהֹג בָּהֶן בְּבֵינוֹנִית... וְהוּא גֹּבַהּ לֵב"
   - Explanation: Rambam says arrogance is not handled by a balanced middle
     path; a person must move far away from it.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Human_Dispositions.2.3
   - Linked question: Amitai #43, whether pride is compatible with greatness.

5. Citation: Ecclesiastes 7:20
   - Hebrew excerpt: "אֵין צַדִּיק בָּאָרֶץ אֲשֶׁר יַעֲשֶׂה־טּוֹב וְלֹא יֶחֱטָא"
   - Explanation: A tzaddik can still have a fault. This helps answer the nuance:
     a person may remain a tzaddik while still needing to fix gaavah.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Ecclesiastes.7.20
   - Linked question: Amitai #43, tzaddikim can still need growth.

### Optional Follow-Up

1. Read Numbers 12:3 and Rambam Deot 2:3. What kind of greatness does Moshe
   show?
2. Read Proverbs 16:5 and Sotah 5a:10. Why is gaavah treated so strongly?
3. Write one example of knowing you are good at something without becoming
   arrogant.

## Question #66 - Huda Weber

- Question: "Why is it more important for children to pray?"
- Topic: children's prayers and Torah.
- Transcript source: content job #26, "Serving Hashem: Joy and Worldly Pleasure
  Debate."
- Short answer: I found verified sources for the special power of children's
  pure Torah and speech, not a clean source saying adults' tefillah is less
  important. The careful answer is that children have a special purity and
  chinuch power, while every Jew's tefillah still matters.

### Sources

1. Citation: Psalms 8:3
   - Hebrew excerpt: "מִפִּי עוֹלְלִים וְיֹנְקִים יִסַּדְתָּ עֹז"
   - Explanation: Tehillim says Hashem establishes strength from the mouths of
     infants and children. This supports the idea that children's voices have a
     special force.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.8.3
   - Linked question: Huda #66, children's prayer.

2. Citation: Shabbat 119b:10
   - Hebrew excerpt: "אֵין הָעוֹלָם מִתְקַיֵּים אֶלָּא בִּשְׁבִיל הֶבֶל תִּינוֹקוֹת שֶׁל בֵּית רַבָּן"
   - Explanation: Chazal say the world endures through the breath of schoolchildren,
     and explain that their breath has no sin. This is the strongest verified
     source for the class idea.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.119b.10
   - Linked question: Huda #66, why children's Torah/prayer is powerful.

3. Citation: Shabbat 119b:8
   - Hebrew excerpt: "לֹא חָרְבָה יְרוּשָׁלַיִם אֶלָּא בִּשְׁבִיל שֶׁבִּיטְּלוּ בָּהּ תִּינוֹקוֹת שֶׁל בֵּית רַבָּן"
   - Explanation: The Gemara treats children's learning as a pillar of the
     community, showing why a child's avodah is not "small."
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.119b.8
   - Linked question: Huda #66, value of children's avodah.

4. Citation: Deuteronomy 4:7
   - Hebrew excerpt: "כַּיהֹוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ בְּכׇל־קׇרְאֵנוּ אֵלָיו"
   - Explanation: Hashem is close whenever we call Him. This keeps the answer
     balanced: children's tefillah is special, but all real tefillah goes to
     Hashem.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.4.7
   - Linked question: Huda #66, prayer reaches Hashem.

### Optional Follow-Up

1. Read Shabbat 119b:10 and underline "hevel she-ein bo chet."
2. Write why a child's Torah or tefillah might affect the whole world.
3. Ask a parent which tefillah they said as a child and still remember.

## Question #67 - Eitan Chaim Golombo

- Question as recorded: "What is the role of the coastal?"
- Transcript caution: the term "coastal" is unclear. In context it may have
  meant "Kosel/Kotel" or another prayer-related word. Do not present that term
  as verified without audio review.
- Transcript source: content job #26.
- Short answer if the term meant Kotel/Jerusalem: Prayer is to Hashem, not to a
  wall or intermediary. The Mikdash/Jerusalem is the place and direction toward
  which Jewish prayer is focused; Hashem hears from heaven. Bar mitzvah changes
  personal mitzvah responsibility, not the address of prayer.

### Sources

1. Citation: I Kings 8:29
   - Hebrew excerpt: "לִשְׁמֹעַ אֶל־הַתְּפִלָּה... אֶל־הַמָּקוֹם הַזֶּה"
   - Explanation: Shlomo asks Hashem to attend to prayers directed toward the
     Beit Hamikdash.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/I_Kings.8.29
   - Linked question: Eitan #67, possible Kotel/Mikdash role.

2. Citation: I Kings 8:30
   - Hebrew excerpt: "וְאַתָּה תִּשְׁמַע אֶל־מְקוֹם שִׁבְתְּךָ אֶל־הַשָּׁמַיִם"
   - Explanation: Even when prayer is directed toward the Mikdash, Hashem hears
     from heaven. The place focuses the prayer; it is not the recipient.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/I_Kings.8.30
   - Linked question: Eitan #67, prayer direction versus recipient.

3. Citation: Berakhot 30a:8
   - Hebrew excerpt: "יְכַוֵּין לִבּוֹ כְּנֶגֶד אָבִיו שֶׁבַּשָּׁמַיִם"
   - Explanation: If a person cannot face the right direction, the core is to
     direct the heart to our Father in Heaven.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.30a.8
   - Linked question: Eitan #67, what prayer is aimed at.

4. Citation: Berakhot 30a:9-10
   - Hebrew excerpt: "יְכַוֵּין אֶת לִבּוֹ כְּנֶגֶד אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל... תֵּל שֶׁכָּל פִּיּוֹת פּוֹנִים בּוֹ"
   - Explanation: Chazal map prayer direction toward Eretz Yisrael, Jerusalem,
     the Mikdash, and the Holy of Holies; the Temple Mount is the place toward
     which all mouths turn.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.30a.9
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.30a.10
   - Linked question: Eitan #67, possible Kotel/Jerusalem direction.

5. Citation: Bereshit Rabbah 63:10
   - Hebrew excerpt: "צָרִיךְ אָדָם לְהִטָּפֵל בִּבְנוֹ עַד י״ג שָׁנָה"
   - Explanation: The class moved into bar mitzvah responsibility. This midrash
     is the source for the father's "baruch shepetarani" language at age 13.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Bereshit_Rabbah.63.10
   - Linked question: Eitan #67, under/over bar mitzvah responsibility.

6. Citation: Magen Avraham 225:5
   - Hebrew excerpt: "עַד עַכְשָׁיו נֶעֱנָשׁ הָאָב כְּשֶׁחָטָא הַבֵּן בִּשְׁבִיל שֶׁלֹּא חִנְּכוֹ"
   - Explanation: Magen Avraham explains the responsibility shift behind the
     blessing, useful for the class distinction between a child and bar mitzvah.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Magen_Avraham.225.5
   - Linked question: Eitan #67, bar mitzvah responsibility.

### Optional Follow-Up

1. Replay the audio and confirm whether "coastal" meant Kosel/Kotel, kisei, or
   something else.
2. If it meant Kotel, read I Kings 8:30 and Berakhot 30a:8-10. Where is prayer
   directed, and Who hears it?
3. Ask what changes at bar mitzvah: the path of prayer, or the responsibility
   of the person praying?

## Question #70 - Menachem Mendel Dratler

- Question: "How can I know the truth about killing someone? Is it allowed if
  the person did not do anything to me?"
- Topic: moral and halachic decision-making.
- Transcript source: content job #30, "Dangerous Black Balloons Threatening
  Israel."
- Short answer: The Torah gives a direct prohibition against murder and grounds
  human life in tzelem Elokim. When a case is hard, the Torah directs us to
  judges and Torah authority, not private impulse or a person who says "yes."

### Sources

1. Citation: Exodus 20:13
   - Hebrew excerpt: "לֹא תִּרְצָח"
   - Explanation: The Aseret HaDibrot directly prohibit murder. This is the
     starting point for the question.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.20.13
   - Linked question: Menachem #70, killing without cause.

2. Citation: Genesis 9:6
   - Hebrew excerpt: "כִּי בְּצֶלֶם אֱלֹהִים עָשָׂה אֶת־הָאָדָם"
   - Explanation: Human life is sacred because people are created in the image
     of God. This gives a deep reason not to treat killing as personal choice.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.9.6
   - Linked question: Menachem #70, why murder is wrong.

3. Citation: Mishneh Torah, Murderer and the Preservation of Life 1:1
   - Hebrew excerpt: "כָּל הוֹרֵג נֶפֶשׁ בֶּן אָדָם עוֹבֵר בְּלֹא תַּעֲשֶׂה"
   - Explanation: Rambam codifies the prohibition as applying to killing a human
     being.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Murderer_and_the_Preservation_of_Life.1.1
   - Linked question: Menachem #70, halachic rule.

4. Citation: Mishneh Torah, Murderer and the Preservation of Life 1:4
   - Hebrew excerpt: "שֶׁאֵין נַפְשׁוֹ... אֶלָּא קִנְיַן הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא"
   - Explanation: Rambam explains that a person's life belongs to Hashem, not to
     another person to forgive or dispose of.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Murderer_and_the_Preservation_of_Life.1.4
   - Linked question: Menachem #70, why private permission is not enough.

5. Citation: Deuteronomy 17:8-11
   - Hebrew excerpt: "כִּי יִפָּלֵא מִמְּךָ דָבָר... וְדָרַשְׁתָּ... עַל־פִּי הַתּוֹרָה אֲשֶׁר יוֹרוּךָ"
   - Explanation: For hard judgment questions, the Torah directs a person to
     the proper judges/teachers and to act by Torah instruction.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.17.8
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.17.9
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.17.10
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.17.11
   - Linked question: Menachem #70, how to decide truth.

6. Citation: Pirkei Avot 1:16
   - Hebrew excerpt: "עֲשֵׂה לְךָ רַב, וְהִסְתַּלֵּק מִן הַסָּפֵק"
   - Explanation: Avot gives a practical decision rule: get a rav/teacher and
     move away from unresolved doubt.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.16
   - Linked question: Menachem #70, personal decision-making.

### Optional Follow-Up

1. Read Genesis 9:6. What does tzelem Elokim change about how we look at another
   person?
2. Read Deuteronomy 17:8-11. What should a person do when a serious decision is
   too hard?
3. Write the difference between "I found someone who agrees with me" and "I
   asked the right Torah authority."

## Question #71 - Hillel Baraka

- Question: "What does it mean that when you read the Torah, you are calling
  Hashem closer to you?"
- Topic: Torah reading, prayer, and spiritual closeness.
- Transcript source: content job #30.
- Short answer: The root kuf-resh-aleph can mean reading and calling. The class
  connected that to prayer language: we prepare to meet Hashem, Hashem is close
  when we call Him, and Torah learning brings Shechinah into the learning space.

### Sources

1. Citation: Amos 4:12
   - Hebrew excerpt: "הִכּוֹן לִקְרַאת־אֱלֹהֶיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל"
   - Explanation: The pasuk uses likrat, "toward meeting," which the class
     discussed in relation to preparing for Hashem.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Amos.4.12
   - Linked question: Hillel #71, calling/meeting Hashem.

2. Citation: Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 12:1
   - Hebrew excerpt: "פֵּרוּשׁ שֶׁיָּכִין אֶת עַצְמוֹ לִפְנֵי הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ"
   - Explanation: Kitzur explains "prepare to meet your God" as preparing
     respectfully before tefillah, like standing before an important minister.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Kitzur_Shulchan_Arukh.12.1
   - Linked question: Hillel #71, preparing to approach Hashem.

3. Citation: Deuteronomy 4:7
   - Hebrew excerpt: "בְּכׇל־קׇרְאֵנוּ אֵלָיו"
   - Explanation: Hashem is close whenever we call Him. This supports Hillel's
     idea that calling is about relationship and closeness, not physical
     distance.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.4.7
   - Linked question: Hillel #71, calling Hashem close.

4. Citation: Pirkei Avot 3:6
   - Hebrew excerpt: "אֲפִלּוּ אֶחָד... אָבֹא אֵלֶיךָ וּבֵרַכְתִּיךָ"
   - Explanation: Avot teaches that even one person learning Torah can draw
     Hashem's presence, based on "in every place where My name is mentioned."
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.3.6
   - Linked question: Hillel #71, Torah learning and Shechinah.

5. Citation: Zohar, Beshalach 4
   - Hebrew excerpt: "כַּמָּה חֲבִיבָא אוֹרַיְיתָא... דְּכָל מַאן דְּאִשְׁתָּדַל בְּאוֹרַיְיתָא"
   - Explanation: The Zohar describes the love of Torah and Hashem listening to
     one who studies it. This is an advanced source for the spiritual-closeness
     idea.
   - Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar,_Beshalach.4
   - Linked question: Hillel #71, Torah and closeness to Hashem.

### Optional Follow-Up

1. Read Deuteronomy 4:7 and underline "bekhol koreinu eilav."
2. Read Pirkei Avot 3:6. What changes when one person learns Torah?
3. Explain in your own words the difference between Hashem being everywhere and
   feeling close to Hashem through Torah.
