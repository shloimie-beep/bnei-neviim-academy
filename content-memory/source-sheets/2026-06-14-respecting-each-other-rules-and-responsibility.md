# Respecting Each Other, Rules, And Responsibility Source Sheets

This packet is class prep for the current BNA rules/respect conversation:
why social rules matter, how boys should treat one another, how to correct
someone without humiliating him, and how disagreement can become Torah instead
of fighting.

It is source research for teaching, not final psak. Practical punishment,
consequence, and individualized chinuch decisions remain for Shloimie/rav
review.

## What This Answers

Transcript anchor:

- #52, "Rules and Responsibility": why a dynamic group with different ages,
  abilities, strengths, and weak spots needs shared rules, roles, and cause and
  effect.
- #42, "Why Kids Bicker and Fight": boys often need direct teaching in how to
  speak, play, argue, and repair.
- #40, "Mixed-Age Learning": older and younger boys learn maturity by treating
  each other respectfully in one shared environment.
- #9 and the June 9 source sheets: lashon hara, free choice, and related
  student questions already have broader source work.

Class questions:

- What does Torah mean by "respecting each other"?
- Is the main rule "do not hurt people," or "actively love people"?
- Why are words, teasing, old insults, and public embarrassment treated as real
  damage?
- How do we correct a friend without shaming him?
- Why can two boys disagree strongly without fighting?
- Why does a mixed-age learning group need clear social rules?

| Topic | Main sources | Use in class |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Disagree without fighting | Avos 5:17; Eruvin 13b; Mishlei 3:17; Tehillim 34:15 | Show that machlokes can be holy when the goal is truth, humility, and peace. |
| Class rules and roles | All sources above | Present rules as the structure that protects learning, relationships, and responsibility. |

## Sheet 1 - The Two-Line Rule: Do Not Hurt, And Actively Love

### Class focus

Use this section first. Ask the boys to build the rule from the inside:

1. What do you hate when someone does it to you?
2. What would it look like not to do that to someone else?
3. What would it look like to go further and actively treat him with love?

### Source 1.1 - Love Your Fellow As Yourself

- Citation: Leviticus 19:18.
- Hebrew excerpt: "וְאָהַבְתָּ לְרֵעֲךָ כָּמוֹךָ אֲנִי יְהֹוָה".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.19.18>
- Main point: Torah does not stop at "do not be mean." It asks a person to care
  about another Jew's dignity and good as something close to his own.
- Answers: What is the positive Torah source for respecting each other?
- Class line: "If I would want someone to care how I feel, I have to train
  myself to care how he feels."

### Source 1.2 - Hillel's Reverse Rule

- Citation: Shabbat 31a:6.
- Hebrew/Aramaic excerpt: "דעלך סני לחברך לא תעביד".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.31a.6>
- Main point: Hillel teaches the convert the whole Torah on one foot: what is
  hateful to you, do not do to your friend; the rest needs learning.
- Answers: How do we turn "respect" into a rule a boy can remember in the
  moment?
- Class line: "Before you say it, grab it, block it, mock it, or interrupt it,
  ask: would I want this done to me?"

### Source 1.3 - Rambam: Love Means Speech, Honor, And Care

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Human Dispositions 6:3.
- Hebrew excerpt: "מִצְוָה עַל כָּל אָדָם לֶאֱהֹב אֶת כָּל אֶחָד וְאֶחָד מִיִּשְׂרָאֵל כְּגוּפוֹ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Human_Dispositions.6.3>
- Main point: Rambam makes the pasuk practical: speak in another person's
  praise, guard his honor, and care about his property the way you care about
  your own.
- Answers: Does "love your fellow" include how we talk and how we treat things
  that belong to someone else?
- Class line: "Respect is not only feelings. It shows up in words, tone,
  another person's space, and another person's things."

### Source 1.4 - Your Friend's Honor Should Matter To You

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 2:10.
- Hebrew excerpt: "יְהִי כְבוֹד חֲבֵרְךָ חָבִיב עָלֶיךָ כְּשֶׁלָּךְ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.2.10>
- Main point: A boy should treat his friend's honor as dear to him as his own;
  the mishnah continues with a warning not to be easily angered.
- Answers: Why does anger ruin respect so quickly?
- Class line: "The test is not whether you care about kavod when calm. The test
  is whether his kavod still matters when you are annoyed."

## Sheet 2 - Every Boy's Kavod

### Class focus

Use this section for the mixed-age group and different-skill-set conversation.
The point is not that everyone is the same. The point is that differences do not
erase kavod.

### Source 2.1 - A Person Is Created In The Image Of Hashem

- Citation: Genesis 1:27.
- Hebrew excerpt: "וַיִּבְרָא אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָאָדָם בְּצַלְמוֹ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.1.27>
- Main point: Human dignity starts before talents, age, skill, marks, or social
  status. A person carries tzelem Elokim.
- Answers: Why does even the weaker or more difficult boy need to be treated
  seriously?
- Class line: "You do not decide whether another person has dignity. Hashem put
  it there."

### Source 2.2 - Avot: Beloved Is Man Created In The Image

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 3:14.
- Hebrew excerpt: "חָבִיב אָדָם שֶׁנִּבְרָא בְצֶלֶם".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.3.14>
- Main point: Avot turns tzelem Elokim into a middos lens: a person should look
  at another human being as beloved before judging usefulness or annoyance.
- Answers: How do we train the boys to see a person before they see the
  problem?
- Class line: "A boy can be annoying and still be beloved. The rule is to deal
  with the behavior without erasing the person."

### Source 2.3 - Love The Stranger

- Citation: Leviticus 19:34.
- Hebrew excerpt: "וְאָהַבְתָּ לוֹ כָּמוֹךָ כִּי־גֵרִים הֱיִיתֶם".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.19.34>
- Main point: Torah singles out the person who is socially vulnerable or does
  not fully belong yet. The group must be careful with the one who feels
  outside.
- Answers: What about a newer boy, younger boy, or boy who is not yet good at
  the group culture?
- Class line: "The Torah cares especially about the person who is easy to make
  feel outside."

### Source 2.4 - Be Students Of Aharon

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 1:12.
- Hebrew excerpt: "אוֹהֵב שָׁלוֹם וְרוֹדֵף שָׁלוֹם, אוֹהֵב אֶת הַבְּרִיּוֹת".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.12>
- Main point: Aharon's path is active peace-making, loving people, and bringing
  them close to Torah.
- Answers: Is peace passive, or is it something a mature boy pursues?
- Class line: "A leader in this room is not the loudest boy. A leader makes the
  group more peaceful and more able to learn."

## Sheet 3 - Words That Hurt Are Real Damage

### Class focus

Use this section to explain why teasing, public comments, old stories, and
verbal pressure are not "just words." Torah treats speech as action.

### Source 3.1 - Do Not Wrong One Another

- Citation: Leviticus 25:17.
- Hebrew excerpt: "וְלֹא תוֹנוּ אִישׁ אֶת־עֲמִיתוֹ וְיָרֵאתָ מֵאֱלֹהֶיךָ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.25.17>
- Main point: The pasuk warns against causing another Jew pain, and attaches
  yirat Hashem because the pain can be hidden from other people.
- Answers: Why are private jabs and "I was only joking" comments serious?
- Class line: "If only you and Hashem know how much you meant to hurt him, that
  is exactly why the pasuk says to fear Hashem."

### Source 3.2 - Rashi: This Includes Painful Words

- Citation: Rashi on Leviticus 25:17.
- Hebrew excerpt: "כָּאן הִזְהִיר עַל אוֹנָאַת דְּבָרִים, שֶׁלֹּא יַקְנִיט אֶת חֲבֵרוֹ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Leviticus.25.17>
- Main point: Rashi explains that the warning includes verbal mistreatment:
  needling, provoking, and giving bad advice for the speaker's benefit.
- Answers: Does Torah have a category for pushing someone's buttons?
- Class line: "Getting a reaction out of someone is not a game if the point is
  to pain him."

### Source 3.3 - Gemara: The Pasuk Speaks About Words

- Citation: Bava Metzia 58b:7.
- Hebrew excerpt: "בְּאוֹנָאַת דְּבָרִים הַכָּתוּב מְדַבֵּר".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Metzia.58b.7>
- Main point: Chazal identify this pasuk specifically with verbal mistreatment.
- Answers: Where do we see clearly that speech can be a Torah-level issue?
- Class line: "Words are not floating air. Words can be a mitzvah, and words
  can be an aveirah."

### Source 3.4 - Do Not Bring Up Someone's Old Pain

- Citation: Bava Metzia 58b:8.
- Hebrew excerpt: "אַל יֹאמַר לוֹ: זְכוֹר מַעֲשֶׂיךָ הָרִאשׁוֹנִים".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Metzia.58b.8>
- Main point: Chazal give examples of forbidden verbal mistreatment, including
  reminding a person of an earlier stage or background in a way that wounds.
- Answers: Why are old embarrassing stories, labels, and "remember when you..."
  comments against the class rules?
- Class line: "If he is trying to grow, you do not pull him backward with your
  mouth."

### Source 3.5 - Verbal Pain Can Be Worse Than Money Pain

- Citation: Bava Metzia 58b:11.
- Hebrew excerpt: "גָּדוֹל אוֹנָאַת דְּבָרִים מֵאוֹנָאַת מָמוֹן".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Metzia.58b.11>
- Main point: Rabbi Yochanan says verbal mistreatment is more severe than
  monetary mistreatment in a key way, because it reaches the person himself.
- Answers: Why do the rules spend so much attention on speech?
- Class line: "A broken pencil can be replaced. A crushed feeling may stay with
  a person."

### Source 3.6 - Public Embarrassment

- Citation: Bava Metzia 58b:12.
- Hebrew excerpt: "כׇּל הַמַּלְבִּין פְּנֵי חֲבֵירוֹ בָּרַבִּים".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Metzia.58b.12>
- Main point: Chazal describe public humiliation with extreme severity.
- Answers: Why should correction, jokes, and teasing avoid making a boy look
  small in front of everyone?
- Class line: "If the whole room laughs and one boy shrinks, that is not a
  harmless moment."

### Source 3.7 - Do Not Be A Social Bystander

- Citation: Leviticus 19:16.
- Hebrew excerpt: "לֹא־תֵלֵךְ רָכִיל בְּעַמֶּיךָ לֹא תַעֲמֹד עַל־דַּם רֵעֶךָ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.19.16>
- Main point: The pasuk places harmful speech and standing by when another is
  hurt next to each other. For class, this supports not joining a mocking
  moment and not silently feeding it.
- Answers: What should a bystander do when the group starts embarrassing
  someone?
- Class line: "You do not have to become a hero speechmaker. You can stop
  laughing, change the topic, or say: enough."

## Sheet 4 - Correcting Behavior Without Shaming

### Class focus

Use this section when explaining consequences, conflict guides, and rules. The
goal is not to pretend nothing happened. The goal is to fix it in a way that
does not destroy the relationship.

### Source 4.1 - Rebuke Without Carrying Sin

- Citation: Leviticus 19:17.
- Hebrew excerpt: "הוֹכֵחַ תּוֹכִיחַ אֶת־עֲמִיתֶךָ וְלֹא־תִשָּׂא עָלָיו חֵטְא".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.19.17>
- Main point: Torah commands correction, but the same pasuk warns not to carry
  sin because of how correction is done.
- Answers: Why are class rules not the same as pretending everything is fine?
- Class line: "Torah says to fix problems. It also says not to fix them by
  creating a new problem."

### Source 4.2 - Rambam: Do Not Hate Silently

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Human Dispositions 6:6.
- Hebrew excerpt: "כְּשֶׁיֶּחְטָא אִישׁ לְאִישׁ לֹא יִשְׂטְמֶנּוּ וְיִשְׁתֹּק".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Human_Dispositions.6.6>
- Main point: If someone hurts you, the first Torah path is not silent hatred;
  it is telling him what happened and giving him a path to repair.
- Answers: What should a boy do instead of holding a grudge or exploding later?
- Class line: "The mature move is not swallowing resentment. It is saying the
  truth calmly enough that repair is possible."

### Source 4.3 - Rambam: Bring Him Back To The Good Path

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Human Dispositions 6:7.
- Hebrew excerpt: "מִצְוָה לְהַחֲזִירוֹ לַמּוּטָב".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Human_Dispositions.6.7>
- Main point: The purpose of rebuke is to help the other person return to a
  better path, not to win, punish, or embarrass.
- Answers: What is the goal when the Rebbe or another boy corrects behavior?
- Class line: "Correction is successful if the person can come back to the
  group better, not smaller."

### Source 4.4 - Rambam: Do Not Humiliate In Rebuke

- Citation: Mishneh Torah, Human Dispositions 6:8.
- Hebrew excerpt: "לֹא יְדַבֵּר לוֹ קָשׁוֹת עַד שֶׁיַּכְלִימֶנּוּ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Human_Dispositions.6.8>
- Main point: Rambam states the rebuke must not be delivered in a harsh way
  that shames the other person.
- Answers: Why should correction usually be calm, direct, and private when
  possible?
- Class line: "If your correction makes him feel crushed, you may have become
  part of the aveirah."

## Sheet 5 - Disagreeing Without Fighting

### Class focus

Use this when boys argue over games, roles, fairness, ideas, or learning. The
class should hear that disagreement is not bad. Ego-driven fighting is the
problem.

### Source 5.1 - Machlokes For Heaven

- Citation: Pirkei Avot 5:17.
- Hebrew excerpt: "כָּל מַחֲלֹקֶת שֶׁהִיא לְשֵׁם שָׁמַיִם, סוֹפָהּ לְהִתְקַיֵּם".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.5.17>
- Main point: Avot distinguishes a disagreement for truth from a disagreement
  driven by ego and power.
- Answers: Can two boys disagree and both still be acting like Torah people?
- Class line: "The question is not only who is right. The question is what you
  are fighting for."

### Source 5.2 - These And Those Are Words Of The Living God

- Citation: Eruvin 13b:10.
- Hebrew excerpt: "אֵלּוּ וָאֵלּוּ דִּבְרֵי אֱלֹהִים חַיִּים".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Eruvin.13b.10>
- Main point: Beis Hillel and Beis Shammai disagreed for years, yet both sides
  were treated as part of Torah.
- Answers: How can a boy respect a different opinion while still arguing his
  side?
- Class line: "If you only know how to respect people who agree with you, you
  have not learned this sugya yet."

### Source 5.3 - Beis Hillel Won Because Of Humility

- Citation: Eruvin 13b:11.
- Hebrew excerpt: "מִפְּנֵי שֶׁנּוֹחִין וַעֲלוּבִין הָיוּ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Eruvin.13b.11>
- Main point: The halacha followed Beis Hillel because they were gentle and
  humble, and they taught Beis Shammai's words before their own.
- Answers: What makes a disagreement mature?
- Class line: "A strong thinker can say the other side clearly before answering
  it."

### Source 5.4 - Torah's Ways Are Pleasantness And Peace

- Citation: Proverbs 3:17.
- Hebrew excerpt: "דְּרָכֶיהָ דַרְכֵי־נֹעַם וְכׇל־נְתִיבוֹתֶיהָ שָׁלוֹם".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.17>
- Main point: Torah is not meant to create a harsh social atmosphere; its paths
  are pleasantness and peace.
- Answers: How should a Torah learning environment feel when it is working?
- Class line: "If our learning makes us sharper but less peaceful, something in
  the learning is not landing yet."

### Source 5.5 - Seek Peace And Pursue It

- Citation: Psalms 34:15.
- Hebrew excerpt: "בַּקֵּשׁ שָׁלוֹם וְרׇדְפֵהוּ".
- Sefaria: <https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.34.15>
- Main point: Peace is not only the absence of a fight. It is something a person
  seeks and pursues.
- Answers: What does a boy do after a conflict instead of just "being done"?
- Class line: "After a fight, peace is not waiting around. Peace is repair."

## Ready Class Flow

### Opening - 5 minutes

Write two lines on the board:

1. What you hate, do not do to someone else.
2. Love your fellow as yourself.

Ask:

- Which one is easier?
- Which one is stronger?
- Why do we need both?

### Source learning - 15 minutes

Learn in this order:

1. Shabbat 31a:6 - Hillel's short rule.
2. Leviticus 19:18 - the positive mitzvah.
3. Pirkei Avot 2:10 - my friend's kavod matters like mine.
4. Rashi on Leviticus 25:17 - words can be ona'ah.

Keep the board simple:

- "Would I want this?"
- "Does this protect his kavod?"
- "Are my words building or hurting?"

### Group rules - 15 minutes

Build the BNA rules from the sources:

| Rule | Source backbone | Class language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No mocking or name-calling | Vayikra 25:17; Bava Metzia 58b | Do not use words to push, sting, or embarrass. |
| No bringing up old embarrassing stories | Bava Metzia 58b:8 | If he is growing, do not pull him backward. |
| No public embarrassment | Bava Metzia 58b:12; Rambam Deot 6:8 | Fix the issue without making a show. |
| Ask before touching or taking | Rambam Deot 6:3 | Care about his things the way you care about yours. |
| One person speaks at a time | Avot 2:10; Eruvin 13b:11 | His words deserve space, not trampling. |
| Disagree with the idea, not the person | Avot 5:17; Eruvin 13b | Machlokes for truth is different from fighting for ego. |
| Protect younger/newer/different boys | Bereishis 1:27; Vayikra 19:34 | The group is judged by how it treats the vulnerable person. |
| Repair after harm | Rambam Deot 6:6-8; Tehillim 34:15 | Tell the truth, apologize when needed, and pursue peace. |

### Practice - 10 minutes

Give scenarios:

1. A boy loses a game and another boy says, "You always ruin it."
   - Which source applies?
   - What could he say instead?
2. A younger boy keeps interrupting.
   - How do you correct without embarrassing?
   - What should the older boy's role be?
3. Two boys disagree about a rule in a game.
   - Is this machlokes l'shem shamayim or ego?
   - Can each side explain the other side first?
4. Someone says, "I was only joking."
   - What does ona'at devarim add to the conversation?

### Closing - 3 minutes

Have each boy choose one line:

- "This week I will protect someone's kavod by..."
- "One thing I hate when done to me that I will not do to others is..."
- "One way I can disagree without fighting is..."

## Short Student Handout Version

### BNA Respect Rules From Torah

1. Before I act, I ask: would I want this done to me?
   - Source: Shabbat 31a:6.
2. I care about another boy's kavod like my own.
   - Source: Pirkei Avot 2:10.
3. I do not use words to sting, embarrass, or bring up old pain.
   - Sources: Leviticus 25:17; Bava Metzia 58b.
4. If someone did something wrong, I try to correct without shaming.
   - Sources: Leviticus 19:17; Rambam Deot 6:8.
5. I can argue strongly about the truth without fighting for ego.
   - Sources: Pirkei Avot 5:17; Eruvin 13b.
6. I actively seek peace and repair after conflict.
   - Source: Psalms 34:15.
