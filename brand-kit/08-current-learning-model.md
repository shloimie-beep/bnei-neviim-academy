# Current Learning Model

Status: curated seed

Use this file as the current BNA learning-model note for brand, parent copy,
student copy, and agent planning. It replaces older family-accountability and
generic secular-curriculum planning language.

## Current Frame

- BNA is a home-based Whole Child Torah Learning Community in Beit Shemesh.
- The current practical learning window is 10:00 to 1:00.
- The center of the day is Torah learning, guided by Rabbi Shloimie, with
  private coaching/check-ins and parent partnership around each boy.
- BNA is not a traditional school, not a full-day secular-studies program, and
  not the old family-accountability app.

## Philosophy To Preserve

- Torah learning should meet the whole child: mind, heart, body, middos,
  confidence, curiosity, responsibility, and relationship with Hashem.
- Autonomy does not mean no structure. It means boys have meaningful space to
  choose, ask, fail, recover, take responsibility, and grow inside clear roles
  and expectations.
- Responsibility grows from meaningful work and roles, not from lectures alone.
  Use the frame of autonomy, mastery, and purpose when writing about student
  responsibility.
- Connection comes before correction. Rules and consequences should protect the
  learning environment and clarify cause and effect, not create shame or a
  power struggle.
- BNA should coach boys toward self-knowledge: noticing what helps them focus,
  what drains them, what they want to master, and what kind of person they are
  becoming.

## Real-World Skills Boundary

- Practical skills, AI tools, worksheets, English, math, media, business, or
  other real-world projects can support the model when they serve the child and
  the Torah learning environment.
- Do not present BNA as a standalone outsourced secular-curriculum platform or
  a generic business-apprenticeship school unless Shloimie explicitly changes
  the offer.
- When discussing real-world learning, keep it concrete and grounded: useful
  work, guided practice, reflection, and skill-building.

## Messaging Guardrails

- Say BNA is for boys ready for something more than regular school.
- Avoid defining boys by failure, mismatch, or pathology.
- Avoid making the model sound laissez-faire. Freedom is paired with coaching,
  expectations, and honest feedback.
- Avoid making the model sound institutional. Parents should feel a living
  Torah learning environment, not a school brochure.
